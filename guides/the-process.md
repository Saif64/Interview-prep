# The Interview Process, End to End

Most preparation advice covers one round. This is the whole pipeline, what each stage is
actually filtering for, and where people lose offers they should have won.

---

## The stages

| Stage | Who runs it | What it filters for | Where people lose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Resume screen** | Recruiter / ATS | Keyword match, obvious fit | Vague bullets, no numbers, no artefacts |
| **Recruiter call** | Recruiter | Basic fit, availability, comp range | Naming a salary number too early |
| **Online assessment** | Automated | Can you code at all | Not reading constraints; no edge cases |
| **Technical phone screen** | Engineer | Baseline problem solving | Silence; jumping to code |
| **On-site / final loop** | 3–5 engineers | Depth, design, collaboration | Treating each round as independent |
| **Behavioural / hiring manager** | Manager | Ownership, conflict, judgement | Vague "we" answers with no decisions |
| **Offer & negotiation** | Recruiter | — | Accepting the first number |

Each stage has its own failure mode, and they are mostly *not* about algorithms.

---

## Before you apply

**Fix the resume first.** It gates everything else, and it is the cheapest thing to improve.
See [resume.md](resume.md).

**Decide your numbers before the first call.** A recruiter will ask for your expected
compensation in round one. Work out your floor and your target *now*, while you are calm and
have no offer pressure. See [negotiation.md](negotiation.md).

**Have one artefact.** A repo, a deployed thing, a writeup. Something that makes a claim on
your resume verifiable in thirty seconds.

---

## The recruiter call

Fifteen minutes, low stakes, one trap: **the compensation question**.

> "What are you looking for in terms of compensation?"

Deflect once, politely. If pressed, give a researched range rather than a number, anchored to
market data — not to your current salary:

> "I'd rather learn more about the scope first. Based on what I've seen for this level in
> this market I'd expect somewhere in the range of X to Y, but I'm flexible depending on the
> whole package."

Everything else in this call is logistics. Be pleasant, be organised, ask what the loop looks
like — knowing the format is genuinely useful preparation.

## After the loop

**Follow up once**, briefly, to the recruiter. Not to every interviewer, not twice.

**If you get rejected**, ask for feedback. You will usually get nothing useful, occasionally
something valuable. Ask anyway, and ask when you can reapply — most companies have a cooling
period of 6–12 months.

**If you get an offer**, do not accept on the call. See [negotiation.md](negotiation.md).

## Running several processes at once

The single highest-leverage tactic in the whole pipeline, and it is scheduling, not skill:

- **Batch your applications.** Offers expire; parallel offers are the only real leverage you
  get in a negotiation.
- **Put your top choice slightly later** in the batch. You will interview better after two or
  three real loops than you will cold.
- **Keep a tracker.** Company, stage, date, contact, next action. A spreadsheet is fine. You
  will lose track around company five otherwise, and dropped threads cost real offers.
